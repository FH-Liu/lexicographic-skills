# Import a Tungusic or Mongolic dictionary

Start with the edition, the user’s requested scope and any current editorial decisions for that corpus. Keep one offline register linking source items, reviewed readings, mappings and remaining work. Prepare the whole agreed dictionary or corpus before an authorized upload.

## Agree how the source will be read

Read the alphabet, usage notes, abbreviation lists and some difficult entries together. Identify the headword and citation conventions, scripts, printed pronunciations, dialect labels, homonym numbering and layout of subsidiary entries. Establish how to cite a source item across different page series or digital versions. A workbook row and a printed entry number may refer to different things.

**Ask the user how to encode special symbols found in the source.** Group outstanding choices by field and reuse conventions already confirmed for this import. Preserve the original glyphs and distinguish display spelling from search equivalences. See the [symbol catalogue and conversion options](references/phonetic-symbols.md).

Distinguish a source spelling, a transliteration, a phonetic transcription and a pronunciation aid, including Chinese characters or pinyin supplied as a reading aid. Keep separately printed values paired with their forms. Use the source’s own examples to interpret vowel length, doubling, diacritics, palatalization and labialization. Preserve a cited verb form alongside a prepared stem and attribute any authorized derivation. Where a dictionary gives traditional Mongolian or Manchu script alongside Cyrillic or romanization, retain each attested form with its script, locator and source-assigned function. Use vowel-harmony or suffix patterns to locate doubtful readings, then settle them against the source.

## Transcribe and proofread

- Inventory the body and non-body sections, including preliminaries, grammar sketches, appendices, indexes and bibliography. Transcribe in-scope documentary material to Markdown with edition and page locators. Preserve section order, examples, notes and table relationships. Link images for figures or layouts that need them. An appendix can receive full documentary coverage while contributing zero lexical entries.
- For scans, check each reading against its page image, including PDFs with searchable text. Then proofread from the source pages to the transcript to catch omitted heads, duplicated lines, missing diacritics and misplaced senses. Record which pages and error classes each pass covered. Keep a content hash for each reviewed Markdown section and check it before reusing that review. Recheck changed sections and the plausible earlier extent of a recurring error.
- For digital sources, check encoding, displayed and stored values, repeated identifiers, numbering and alternative-form delimiters against the original. Verify difficult readings in context. Preserve unresolved readings with their alternatives and locators.
- Keep a stable key for each source item through corrections. Record who supplied each field, including translations and editorial inferences. Preserve requested blanks. Leave missing pronunciations, parts of speech and translations empty unless enrichment is authorized. Use spelling correspondences to check printed IPA and label any inferred pronunciation separately. Distinguish uncertainty in decipherment, translation and analysis.

## Map entries and reconcile meanings

Preserve printed organization, homonyms, senses, labels, example translations and the exact form to which a pronunciation belongs. Attach each regional or register label to the form, sense or entry it describes. Preserve the literal abbreviation alongside an attributed interpretation when its reading is uncertain.

In a multilingual comparative wordlist, preserve row and column alignment, printed blanks and the variety of each form. Record the alignment as the compiler’s comparison. Claims of identity, cognacy or borrowing require separate evidence.

Distinguish a conventional expression from a productive phrase or a sentence illustrating usage. Give independently listed examples their own source locators. Follow explicit cross-references to the intended head, homonym and sense. Record a pending target once and revisit it when available. A printed comparison, a see-reference and a translation equivalent convey different information.

Establish the permitted merge scope before consolidating entries across sources. Within an authorized reconciled entry, compare the referent, grammatical function, restrictions and examples. Combine equivalent senses, retaining their attested wordings in each language, contributors, locators, dialect claims and examples. Keep distinct meanings and referrals recoverable. Carry old-to-surviving form and sense mappings through all affected references. Assert a form–sense pairing only where the source supports it and the destination can represent it. Disclose any distinctions the destination cannot represent.

When lexical analysis is requested, pass verified headword groups, sense attachments and example spans to [Families and Loans](../lexicographic-families-and-loans/SKILL.md).

## Work efficiently and deliver

For long tasks, ask the user to allow parallel processing with three subagents unless already authorized. Give each a distinct scope and reconcile the findings in the central register. Work in coherent batches. When repeated setup or verification consumes a large share of the time, increase batch size while preserving both source checks. Use smaller batches for difficult layouts or uncertain readings. Save coverage, unique evidence and the exact next action.

Account for every source item, including exclusions and gaps. For an authorized publication, use the destination’s established guarded workflow and reconcile intervening edits. Check that the intended destination and requested format preserve the reviewed records, attribution and unrelated material. Read back the published result. Update the source description with bibliographic identity, an illustrative page and locator, actual coverage, separate source-item and reconciled-entry counts, and clearly attributed editorial additions. Keep detailed processing history in the register.
